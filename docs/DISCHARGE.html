Removing native_decide from the R2K2ZZN proof

State on 2026-11-02, branch band (worktree ~/claude/R2K2ZZN-band). Measured numbers are marked as measured; everything else is a projection and says so.

Bottom line. The proof in proof/ has no native_decide. Its main results depend only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound (checked with #print axioms). All 154 calls of the released proof are replaced: 144 by 21 kernel-checked box certificates (Theorem A), 6 by a new argument and 4 by 59 kernel-checked window certificates (Theorem B). A clean build takes 5.4 CPU-hours (measured: 19 552 s user + system, 2.6 h wall with two Lean processes, peak 12.4 GB per process), with Mathlib from the cache. certgen/regen.sh regenerates proof/ byte for byte from the committed certificates.

1. What is being proved

An instance is a rectangle of R × C cells with four endpoints s0, t0 (colour 0) and s1, t1 (colour 1). It is solvable when two disjoint paths s0→t0 and s1→t1 together visit every cell. The catalogue is a finite list of forbidden local patterns; an instance passes when none of them occurs.

The main theorem ZZN.passes_iff_solvable says: when both sides are at least 11, an instance is solvable exactly when it passes. It splits into two directions.

Verification scope: the axiom check covers the Lean statements. The C programs and scripts that generate certificates (certgen/, lean/tools/) are not verified; a wrong certificate makes the kernel check fail, so they cannot make a false statement pass.

3. Theorem A: strategy

3.1 Reduce to finitely many boxes

Proved reductions shrink a passing instance while keeping it passing and keeping solvability equivalent. On branch band, band compressions delete two free lines whose neighbourhood is empty; with margin 5 they apply whenever a side has at least 13 lines and the endpoints leave room. What remains are irreducible instances in boxes of sides 11 to 16 (sides 17–22 have none). Up to the 64 symmetries of a square box (32 otherwise: reflections, transposition on squares, swapping colours, reversing a path) there are about 12.06 million such instances, most of them in three boxes:

3.2 Certify each instance with a cut tree

An instance is shown solvable by a cut tree: cut the rectangle along a line, say which side each path's ends go to and where a path crosses the line, recurse, and stop at leaves that hold one path in a rectangle. A leaf is checked by the Itai–Papadimitriou–Szwarcfiter conditions for a Hamiltonian path between two given cells of a rectangle (accB). The checker is Cut.check; solvable_of_check proves it sound.

A template is a cut tree whose positions are given relative to the endpoints and the edges (anchor plus offset), so one template certifies many instances. The C side searches templates; 2281 templates cover every instance of 11 × 11.

3.3 Check many instances at once

A slice fixes the rows of the four endpoints; its instances differ only in their columns. For a box of height h there are h4 column tuples (14 641 for h = 11). The kernel packs one column value per 8-bit lane of a single huge natural number and evaluates a template on all lanes with a fixed number of whole-number operations (SWAR, "SIMD within a register"). Per slice the certificate lists about 30 (template, symmetry) pairs; a lane is certified when one pair checks on it. Masks remove lanes that are not instances of this slice (equal cells, wrong parity, not the symmetry representative).

4. Theorem B: strategy

Theorem B needs: in a solvable instance, none of the window patterns B, C, D occurs. Each pattern lives in a corner window (6 × 6 cells for C6 and B; 6 × 6 or 8 × 8 for D). The proof shows that no way of routing two paths inside the window, with the endpoints the pattern prescribes, can be completed outside it.

The certificate is a dynamic program over the window (WinCheck.lean): cells are added in row order, and a state records how the processed cells are joined into path pieces and where those pieces leave the processed region. The set of reachable states after each cell is given to the kernel as data (a checkpoint, a packed list of state codes). The kernel checks:

  • stepOK: every successor of every state in a checkpoint bucket appears in the next checkpoint (one theorem per stage and bucket, the T modules);
  • finalOK: every state of the last checkpoint fails the outside test, so no completion exists (one theorem per final bucket, the Fin modules).

certify_of_chain turns the chain into the statement. Colour symmetry halves the cases to 59 certificates. The certificates share their late stages (WinKU/Shared), the step and final checks use direct number operations (stepOKD, finalSigOKL), and the 59 case modules are bundled into 6, so Theorem B is 28 modules (it began as 6 061).

6. Why it is slow

The kernel evaluates definitions by rewriting terms. Arithmetic on big numbers is fast (GMP); the cost is the rewriting around it. Measured in this tree:

Kernel workCost
One whole-number operation on a 1 KB number, in straight-line code≈ 3 µs
The same operation's arithmetic on a 100 KB number≈ 0.22 ns per byte
One operation inside the leaf test accS (helper calls, type classes)≈ 8 µs
One lane operation through the LV layer (form dispatch, Option, smallness checks)≈ 90 µs
One step of structural recursion (compiled through brecOn)≈ 44 µs
One step of the same loop written with Nat.rec≈ 8 µs
Importing Mathlib, per module2.8 – 4 s

Three consequences.

  1. Overhead dominates. A template check does a few thousand useful operations, which would take about 5 ms at the straight-line cost; it takes 35–45 ms. Adding lanes is nearly free: four times the lanes costs about 15% more per check.
  2. Memory limits merging. The kernel keeps every intermediate value until a declaration ends, about 470 bytes per lane per check. One slice theorem holds about 1 GB, which caps how much work can share one declaration and with it how far slices can be merged.
  3. Many small modules. Every module pays the Mathlib import. Theorem B first had 6 061 modules and Theorem A 8 slices per module; the final export has 304 modules in all.

8. Release

proof/ is a self-contained Lake project in the Lean module system, generated by lean/tools/make_proof.py. One Palomar registration: Challenge.lean (998 lines, imports only Mathlib) holds solvableBT_main (the decision procedure is correct within Cdec · (R C + R + C + 1) steps), fires3T_iff_solvable (the characterization) and solveZZNT_main (the construction is sound, complete and polynomial), all on the procedures with a step counter. It copies the definitions it needs from ZZN/Statement.lean and ZZN/TimedStatement.lean without their documentation. The plain statements (solvableB_iff, passes_iff_solvable, solveZZN_sound, solveZZN_complete) are proved but not registered; with them the challenge would pass 1 000 lines.

Lean shares matcher helpers between definitions of one module, so a single-file copy can differ from the two-module library. A hash comparison of every definition the registered statements reach (271, matchers included) shows the challenge identical to the library.

Palomar verifies on a 16-core, 32 GB runner within 5.5 hours of wall-clock time (build, then Lean's kernel and nanoda). Locally nanoda accepted the export of the plain statements' solution in 70 minutes on 4 cores. The registered solution was not run through the comparator locally.

9. Terms

Box
A rectangle size w × h (11 ≤ w ≤ h ≤ 16) whose irreducible instances need a certificate.
Irreducible
No proved reduction (strip deletion, band compression) applies.
Slice
All instances of a box with the same four endpoint rows; checked together in one theorem.
Lane
One 8-bit field of a big natural number; one instance per lane.
SWAR
Computing on all lanes at once with ordinary whole-number operations.
Cut tree, leaf
A recursive split of the rectangle by lines; a leaf holds one path and is checked by the IPS conditions.
Template
A cut tree with positions relative to the endpoints and edges, reusable across instances.
Pair
A template together with a symmetry, checked on all lanes of a slice.
Window, checkpoint
For Theorem B, a 6 × 6 corner region and the set of reachable dynamic-programming states after a given cell, supplied as data.
decide +kernel
A proof by evaluation performed by the Lean kernel itself; trusted like any other kernel check.
native_decide
A proof by evaluation in compiled code; adds an axiom per use.
